02 / 11

OpenStax Biology — Cellular respiration
Cellular respiration is the process by which cells harvest energy from food molecules. In eukaryotes it usually means oxidizing glucose to carbon dioxide and water while capturing free energy as ATP. The pathway has three tightly linked stages. Glycolysis occurs in the cytosol and splits one glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, with a small net gain of ATP and NADH. If oxygen is available, pyruvate enters mitochondria and is converted to acetyl CoA, which feeds the citric acid cycle. The citric acid cycle oxidizes the remaining carbons to CO2 and loads electron carriers NADH and FADH2. Oxidative phosphorylation then uses those electrons in the electron transport chain; the resulting proton gradient drives ATP synthase. Most of the ATP from glucose is made here. Without oxygen, cells may stop after glycolysis and ferment pyruvate so NAD+ can be regenerated.
Devon decodes it word by word. Nine sentences, a dozen terms he has to sound out. He is not slow at biology. He is slow at this shape.
Mara loses the thread by sentence three. There is no next action, no edge, and no finish line anywhere on the page.

Our solution
03 / 11
One passage in. A shape you can hold.
ADDY is not a chatbot about your chapter. You hand it a passage; it hands back a restructured artefact built for the learner in front of it — and it tells you, in one line, why it picked that shape.
1
Put anything in
Paste text. Drop a PDF — scanned ones go through vision OCR. A .docx, a public Google Doc, or any article URL. Three passages come preloaded so a demo can never die on a bad paste.
8 MB and 80k characters per reading. Private Google Docs are refused by design.
2
It picks the shape
A classifier reads the material as a process, a rule system, or a definition cluster. Crossed with the learner, that decides the remake — and the answer arrives as a sentence, not a setting.
“I chose a checklist because this is a three-stage process and you asked for one thing at a time.”
3
You push back
The original stays on screen next to the remake, so a human can always check it. When the remake is not landing, one button — This isn’t working — sends the complaint back through the planner.
No format menu to learn. No diagnosis to declare. One button.

Our solution · the loop

The planner picks the shape.
We never ask a student to choose a format they have not seen.
01
Ingest
POST /api/ingest
Extract, classify, chunk. Text, PDF, .docx, a public Google Doc, a URL, or a preloaded sample. Scanned PDFs go through vision OCR.
02
Classify
ingest/classify.js
A cheap classifier runs before the model: process, rule system, or definition cluster. Crossed with the learner mode, that is the decision.
03
Remake
POST /api/chat
DeepSeek V4.1 Flash over OpenRouter. The prompt bans invented facts and holds scientific terms verbatim. No key? A local heuristic still returns a remake.
04
Adapt
POST /api/chat
This isn’t working sends the complaint back through the same endpoint. It can switch the reading tab and re-remake from the same source.
The line that proves it is agentic
Every remake opens by naming its own format and its reason — “I chose a flowchart because…”. A template cannot write that sentence, because a template never made a choice.
One constraint worth knowing
Vercel functions are stateless, so the client holds the parsed document and resends it on every call. A documentId alone will not survive a new instance.

Over-fragmentation
The opposite complaint, and the one an agent tuned only for less at a time gets catastrophically wrong. She has the steps; she has lost the pathway.
Format
Checklist → flowchart. All three stages on one screen. This is the tab switch the button already performs.
Added
The oxygen branch drawn explicitly — the fork that a list can only put in sequence.
Kept
Her three finished steps carry over as ticks on the matching nodes. She does not restart.
Removed
Step gating. Nothing is hidden any more.

One claim, not the page
A comprehension gap on a specific sentence — S8. Rebuilding the whole artefact would be the wrong answer, and would cost her the three steps she has banked.
Format
Checklist → checklist. Only the last step is rebuilt.
Expanded
One step becomes three: the chain needs oxygen → NADH stays loaded without it → glycolysis stalls unless fermentation frees NAD+.
Kept
The other seven steps are untouched, ticks and all.
Flagged
Marked shaky on her profile, so it returns in the recap whether or not she asks.
Every rebuild goes back through the same prompt constraints and the same human read. An agent that answered all three of these by cutting words would eventually cut the biology — which is why the repair is chosen, not defaulted.

Our solution · fidelity
07 / 11
Nothing lost, nothing invented.
A remake is only worth anything if the student can still be examined on it. Here is what enforces that today — and the part we have not earned yet.
Shipped · in the prompt
The model is held to the passage
The remake prompt forbids inventing facts and requires scientific terms survive verbatim — a checklist may not turn oxidative phosphorylation into “the last bit”. Every remake opens by naming the format and the reason.
Shipped · on the screen
A human reads them side by side
The original never leaves the Reading view. A teammate reads source against remake and signs off before we call a reading done. That is the check the brief asked for, and it is the one that actually ran.
Not shipped — and we will say so
The counters are seeded, not computed
The Reading screen renders a supported / missing / invented panel, but those flags are local seed data. There is no server claim map behind them yet. We are not going to show a judge a number our backend did not produce.
The two failures the human is reading for
Lost — the scaffold goes
Summarisers drop the sentence that frames the rest. Cut “the pathway has three tightly linked stages” and a student can finish every step without the structure the exam is actually asking for.
Invented — the plausible number
The passage says a small net gain and most of the ATP is made here. It never counts. A model that helpfully supplies “38 ATP per glucose” has taught something the source did not say.

Our solution · what we assumed
09 / 11
Four assumptions we would defend.
And the claims we will not make.
Source is exam-authoritative
A loose paraphrase can teach a student a fact that is not on the test. We change the shape — flowchart, checklist, quest — never the science. And the original never leaves the screen, so any line in the remake can be put back against the sentence it came from.
We are not diagnosing anyone
The modes encode study costs, not conditions: Mara drops the thread without a next action, Devon pays per word to decode. The app’s own onboarding says it — “No diagnosis needed. This just helps us set things up.” Devon and Mara are composites; we will say so if you ask.
Font tools keep the same paragraph
That is the failure, and it is why a bigger typeface was never going to be enough. Devon needs fewer words and a visible branch. Mara needs one live step and her place held. Opposite repairs, same paragraph — one setting cannot serve both.
The agent will get something wrong
Summarisers drop the scaffold (“three tightly linked stages”) and invent the plausible (“38 ATP per glucose”). The brief asked us to catch that. Today the prompt forbids it, the source sits beside the remake, and a human teammate is the last word — the automated claim map is still on the list.
The scope is small on purpose: one passage at a time, two learner modes, one honest gap list. We would rather be checkable than broad.
