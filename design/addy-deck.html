OpenStax Biology — Cellular respiration
Cellular respiration is the process by which cells harvest energy from food molecules. In eukaryotes it usually means oxidizing glucose to carbon dioxide and water while capturing free energy as ATP. The pathway has three tightly linked stages. Glycolysis occurs in the cytosol and splits one glucose into two molecules of pyruvate, with a small net gain of ATP and NADH. If oxygen is available, pyruvate enters mitochondria and is converted to acetyl CoA, which feeds the citric acid cycle. The citric acid cycle oxidizes the remaining carbons to CO2 and loads electron carriers NADH and FADH2. Oxidative phosphorylation then uses those electrons in the electron transport chain; the resulting proton gradient drives ATP synthase. Most of the ATP from glucose is made here. Without oxygen, cells may stop after glycolysis and ferment pyruvate so NAD+ can be regenerated.
Devon decodes it word by word. Nine sentences, a dozen terms he has to sound out. He is not slow at biology. He is slow at this shape.
Mara loses the thread by sentence three. There is no next action, no edge, and no finish line anywhere on the page.

How it works
04 / 11
The planner decides the shape.
We never ask the student to pick a format they have never seen.
01
Read
Split the source into atomic claims and number them. Nine sentences became S1–S8. Nothing is summarised yet — this is only bookkeeping.
02
Decide
Classify the material (sequential process? decision logic? definition-heavy?) against the learner, and print the reason on screen. Three-stage process with a branch + high decode cost → flowchart. Same passage, low working memory → staged quest.
03
Produce
Build the artefact. Every node, step and card carries the id of the claim it came from — so the next stage has something to check against.
04
Self-check
Re-read the output against the source and answer two questions only: is anything missing, and is anything here the source never said.
05
Adapt
The student is the only judge that counts.
When Mara taps This isn’t working and says why, that is not a support ticket — it is a new input to Decide. The agent re-runs the choice with her complaint added to her profile, rebuilds, and re-checks against the same source. The passage never changes. The shape does.

What we assumed
09 / 11
Four assumptions we would defend.
And the claims we will not make.
Source is exam-authoritative
A loose paraphrase can teach a student a fact that is not on the test. We change the shape — flowchart, checklist, quest — never the science. Every node keeps a claim id back to the sentence it came from, which is what makes the check on slide 05 possible at all.
We are not diagnosing anyone
The modes encode study costs, not conditions: Mara drops the thread without a next action, Devon pays per word to decode. The app’s own onboarding says it — “No diagnosis needed. This just helps us set things up.” Devon and Mara are composites; we will say so if you ask.
Font tools keep the same paragraph
That is the failure, and it is why a bigger typeface was never going to be enough. Devon needs fewer words and a visible branch. Mara needs one live step and her place held. Opposite repairs, same paragraph — one setting cannot serve both.
The agent will get something wrong
Summarisers drop the scaffold (“three tightly linked stages”) and invent the plausible (“38 ATP per glucose”). Ours did both on draft 1. The brief asked us to catch that, so missing and invented are counters in the product, and a human teammate is the last word.
The scope is small on purpose: one exam-grade passage, eight atomic claims, two modes. We would rather be checkable than broad.

Why it matters
10 / 11
Accessibility tools ask the student to keep reading the same shape, harder.
One shape fits nobody
Larger type and text-to-speech help, and every student gets the same one. Devon and Mara need opposite things from the same paragraph — and neither of them needs a bigger font.
The student is the signal
Onboarding asks what trips you up, never what you have been diagnosed with. After that, tapping This isn’t working is the whole interface — and the planner has to do something specific with it.
Traceable enough to assign
An instructor will not hand a class an AI rewrite of the textbook they are examined on. Claim-level tracing is what makes this adoptable instead of a novelty.
The text stays exactly as authoritative as it was. Only the way in changes.
