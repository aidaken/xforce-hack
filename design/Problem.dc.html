Human Physiology, 8e · Ch. 12, p. 340
Thermoregulation in endotherms is maintained by a negative feedback loop. Thermoreceptors distributed in the skin and in the hypothalamus detect deviation from the body’s set point of approximately 37 °C. Afferent signals are integrated by the preoptic area of the hypothalamus, which functions as the control center for the loop. When core temperature rises above the set point, efferent signals produce vasodilation of cutaneous arterioles and activation of the eccrine sweat glands, increasing radiative and evaporative heat loss respectively. When core temperature falls below the set point, the hypothalamus initiates vasoconstriction, piloerection, and shivering thermogenesis, while the thyroid axis may be recruited to sustain nonshivering thermogenesis over longer intervals. Because the corrective response in each case opposes the initial deviation rather than amplifying it, the loop is self-limiting: as core temperature returns toward the set point, receptor firing diminishes and the effector response is progressively withdrawn.
