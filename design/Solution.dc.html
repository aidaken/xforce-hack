One passage in. A shape you can hold.
ADDY is not a chatbot about your chapter. You hand it a passage; it hands back a restructured artefact built for the learner in front of it — and it tells you, in one line, why it picked that shape.
1
Put anything in
Paste text. Drop a PDF — scanned ones go through vision OCR. A .docx, a public Google Doc, or any article URL. Three passages come preloaded so a demo can never die on a bad paste.
8 MB and 80k characters per reading. Private Google Docs are refused by design.
2
It picks the shape
A classifier reads the material as a process, a rule system, or a definition cluster. Crossed with the learner, that decides the remake — and the answer arrives as a sentence, not a setting.
“I chose a checklist because this is a three-stage process and you asked for one thing at a time.”
3
You push back
The original stays on screen next to the remake, so a human can always check it. When the remake is not landing, one button — This isn’t working — sends the complaint back through the planner.
No format menu to learn. No diagnosis to declare. One button.
