# Two learners, actually different

If both remakes are “bullet points with a nicer font,” we fail the judge test. The formats have to *feel* different.

## ADHD mode — reduce working-memory load

Research-shaped, not clinical:

- One idea at a time; visible progress (1/5, 2/5).
- Immediate structure: numbered steps, checkboxes, optional tiny quest (“unlock the Krebs cycle”).
- High signal, low decoration. Motion only to mark “you are here.”
- Time-box language: “do this next,” not “consider the following.”

**Default representation:** checklist of the process, then a 3-card quest if they tap Adapt.

## Dyslexia mode — reduce decoding load

- Short lines, extra letter/word spacing, large type (18–22px body).
- Avoid long paragraph restates of the textbook.
- Keep scientific terms stable; don’t synonym-swap.
- Spatial layout: flowchart / labeled stages, not a wall of prose.
- Dyslexia-friendly font (e.g. Atkinson Hyperlegible or OpenDyslexic) and off-white / cream background, dark text, no pale gray on white.

**Default representation:** 3–5 node flowchart with 3–6 word labels and a one-line caption each.

## Same passage, two skins (demo contract)

Passage: cellular respiration overview.

| | ADHD | Dyslexia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shape | vertical checklist + optional XP | left-to-right flowchart |
| Density | 6–8 short steps | 4 nodes, bigger type |
| Color | bright progress green | high-contrast cream + navy |
| Adapt to | quest cards | even fewer words + read-aloud captions |

## What we will not claim

We are not diagnosing anyone. Copy in the app: “designed around common ADHD / dyslexia study preferences,” not “clinically proven.”
